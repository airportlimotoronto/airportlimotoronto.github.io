Terminal 1 or Terminal 3? What Your Pearson Terminal Changes About Your Ride

Two buildings, one airport, and a few details worth knowing before you land.

Book a Pearson Airport Limo ride and the first question you hear is which terminal. Toronto Pearson has two, they sit apart from each other, and the wrong one costs you a train ride.

Here is what differs between them, and what to tell your driver so nobody circles the airport looking for you.

Which terminal am I using?

Your airline decides, not you. Air Canada and its partner airlines use Terminal 1. WestJet and a long list of other carriers use Terminal 3.

Airlines do move, and shared flights can confuse things. A ticket sold by one airline may be flown by another from the other building. Trust the terminal printed on your booking or boarding pass over anything you remember from last year.

Leaving Toronto: what changes at drop-off

Each terminal has its own departures road and its own row of doors. Tell the driver your airline as well as the terminal, because the right door can save you a long walk with bags.

If you are flying to the United States, you will pass American customs before boarding in either building. Allow for that when you choose your pickup time.

Arriving in Toronto: what changes at pickup

This is where the terminal matters most. Arrivals halls are separate, and a domestic flight and an international flight can exit through different doors of the same building.

With a booked car, the chauffeur meets you inside arrivals holding a sign with your name. Your flight is tracked, so an early or late landing changes the meeting time without a phone call from you.

  • Give the flight number, not only the arrival time. The number tells the driver the terminal and the door.
  • Turn your phone on as soon as you land, in case the driver needs to reach you.
  • Collect your bags first, then look for your name. Meeting before the carousel only splits the group.
  • If two people in your party land at different terminals, say so when booking.

What if I end up at the wrong terminal?

It happens, and it is fixable. A free train runs between Terminal 1 and Terminal 3 all day and night. The ride is short, but add the walk on each end and you should count on about fifteen minutes door to door.

If you are being dropped off and realise the mistake in the car, just say so. It is a short drive between the two buildings, far quicker than taking the train with luggage.

Connecting between the two

Some trips land at one terminal and leave from the other. If your bags are checked through, you only move yourself. If they are not, collect them, take the train, and check in again.

Leave at least ninety minutes for that kind of change. Customs lines are the part nobody can predict.

Does the terminal change the price?

For a flat-rate ride, no. The fare is agreed before you book and it is the same for either terminal. It also holds when traffic is slow or your flight comes in late.

Three things to have ready when you book

  1. Your airline and flight number.
  2. The terminal shown on your ticket.
  3. How many people and how many bags.

With those three details, the terminal stops being a puzzle. The car simply goes to the right door.
