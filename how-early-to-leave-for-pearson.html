How Early Should You Leave for Pearson? A Timing Guide by Time of Day

Work backwards from your flight, not forwards from your alarm.

Drivers at Toronto Airport Limo get asked one thing more than any other: what time should the car come? The honest answer depends on the hour your plane leaves.

Most people guess, add a bit, and hope. A better way is to count backwards in four steps. It takes two minutes and it removes the guessing.

Step one: when must you be inside the terminal?

Airlines commonly ask you to arrive two hours before a flight inside Canada and three hours before an international one. Flights to the United States need extra care, because you clear American customs at Pearson before you board.

Write that arrival time down. Everything else is measured from it, not from the departure time on your ticket.

Step two: how long is the drive at that hour?

The same route can take twice as long depending on the clock. Use the hour you will be on the road, not the hour you are planning.

  • Before 6 a.m.: the highways are mostly clear. The drive is close to its shortest.
  • 6 a.m. to 9:30 a.m. on weekdays: commuter traffic builds fast. Give the drive half as much time again.
  • Midday: steady and fairly easy to predict.
  • 3 p.m. to 7 p.m. on weekdays: the slowest stretch of the day. Plan for the longest drive.
  • Late evening and weekends: usually quick, unless there is roadwork or a big event letting out.

Step three: add a cushion for the things you cannot see

Weather, a closed lane or a stalled truck can each cost twenty minutes. Add a cushion of fifteen minutes on a clear summer morning and thirty or more when snow or heavy rain is forecast.

If you are checking bags, travelling with children or returning a pile of winter coats to a suitcase, add ten more. Small delays at the door add up.

Step four: set the pickup, then stop adjusting it

Subtract the drive and the cushion from your terminal time. That is your pickup time. Book it and leave it alone, because moving it later by ten minutes the night before is how people miss flights.

A worked example

Say your flight to Vancouver leaves at 8:40 on a Tuesday morning. You want to be inside by 6:40. A drive that takes thirty minutes at night should be planned at forty-five for that hour. Add fifteen for the unknown. The car should be at your door at 5:40.

Now move the same flight to 6:30 p.m. The terminal time is 4:30, right in the slow afternoon stretch. Plan the drive at an hour, keep the cushion, and the pickup becomes 3:15. Same flight, same house, a very different morning.

Why a booked car makes the maths easier

With a booked ride the pickup time is fixed and the price is known before you book. It stays the same if traffic crawls, so a slow highway costs you time and nothing more.

Rides run at any hour, to Terminal 1 and Terminal 3 alike, so a 4 a.m. pickup is as normal as a noon one. Tell the company your flight number when you book. It lets them check your timing against the real schedule.

The short version

  1. Find the time you must be inside the terminal.
  2. Estimate the drive for the hour you will actually travel.
  3. Add a cushion for weather and luggage.
  4. Book that pickup time and do not shave it.

Do this once and you will stop asking how early is early enough. You will know.
