Travelling Alone and Landing Late: What to Check Before You Get Into Any Car

A short safety routine for solo travellers arriving in Toronto.

Arriving by yourself feels different from arriving with company. The team at Airport Limo Toronto, a family-owned car service, hears the same worry from solo travellers every week: how do I know the car is really mine?

The answer is a routine of small checks. None of them takes more than a minute, and together they remove nearly all the risk.

Before you fly: set it up while you are calm

Safety on arrival is mostly decided at home. Book the ride ahead and keep the confirmation where you can open it without a signal.

  • Save the company's phone number in your contacts, not only in an email.
  • Screenshot the booking: pickup place, time and the agreed fare.
  • Send the same screenshot to one person who will be awake when you land.
  • Charge your phone and pack a cable in your hand luggage.

In the arrivals hall: let the driver prove it

A booked chauffeur waits inside arrivals with a sign showing your name. That sign is your first check. Walk up to it, and do not announce who you are from across the hall.

Then ask one question: who is this booking for, and where is it going? A real driver reads both from the job sheet. Someone guessing cannot answer.

If a stranger approaches and offers you a ride, say no and keep walking. Drivers with a real booking do not need to hunt for passengers.

At the car: three seconds, three checks

  1. The car matches what the company told you, or the driver explains the change before you ask.
  2. The driver loads the bags while you watch, and you keep your handbag or laptop with you.
  3. You sit in the back seat, behind the passenger side, with your phone in your hand.

On the road: stay visible to someone

Message your contact when you leave the airport. Share your live location if your phone allows it, and say what time you expect to be home.

Keep the map open on your own screen. You are not checking up on anyone. You are simply a passenger who knows where the car is.

If anything feels wrong, say you need to stop at a busy, well-lit place such as a gas station or a hotel entrance. A professional driver will do it without argument.

Why the fare should never be a surprise

Arguments about money are the most common late-night problem. A flat rate ends them before they start, because you know the price before you book and it does not change with traffic or a delayed landing.

Paying by card or debit also helps. It leaves a record, and it means you are not counting cash in a dark driveway.

When your flight is hours late

Delays are when solo travellers improvise, and improvising is when mistakes happen. If your flight is tracked by the car company, the pickup moves with the plane and you still walk out to a name sign.

If you had no booking, call a company from inside the terminal and wait there. A bright hall with staff around is a better waiting room than the curb.

The routine on one line

Book ahead, share the details, make the driver name the booking, sit in the back, and tell someone you are moving. Five habits, and the last stretch of the trip becomes the easy one.
